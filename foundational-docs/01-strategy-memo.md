# Strategy Memo — SwipeJobs Melbourne

## One-Line Pitch

**Swipe-to-apply for fast local Melbourne jobs where speed and availability matter more than resumes.**

## Problem

Melbourne has no fast, mobile-native way for casual workers to find local shifts and for small employers to fill them.

Existing options are either:
- **Too heavy** — SEEK/Indeed/LinkedIn require full profiles, cover letters, and formal applications for even a 4-hour cafe shift.
- **Too fragmented** — Facebook groups, WhatsApp chains, agency texts — messy, inconsistent, hard to track.
- **Too expensive** — Recruitment agencies charge 15-25% of annual salary for casual roles.
- **Too slow** — By the time a candidate hears back, the shift is gone or the employer has moved on.

Casual/shift-based work matching is fundamentally different from professional recruitment. It's about **availability, location, and speed** — not education history, career narrative, or reference checks.

## Target Users

| User | Pain | Current Behaviour |
|------|------|-------------------|
| **Candidate** — Melbourne student, casual worker, hospitality/retail/warehouse worker | Scrolling through 50+ full job ads to find casual shifts; applying formally for every role; missing shifts because response is too slow | Juggling SEEK, Indeed, agency calls, Facebook groups, word-of-mouth |
| **Employer** — Cafe owner, retail manager, event staffing lead, warehouse supervisor | Posting a shift on SEEK costs $300+ and pulls mostly full-time applicants; agencies take a cut; Facebook posts get lost | Agencies, Facebook groups, staff referrals, signs in windows |

## Why Now

1. **Mobile-native job search is still broken.** The big job boards are desktop-first, form-heavy, and optimised for professional recruitment, not casual work.
2. **Melbourne's casual workforce is large and growing.** Hospitality, events, retail, and warehousing all rely on flexible staffing. Post-COVID, shift-based work has only increased.
3. **Swipe UX is proven.** Tinder, Hinge, Bumble have trained a generation to evaluate options quickly through swipes. Applying that to rapidly browsing jobs is low-friction and intuitive.
4. **Small employers are underserved.** Enterprise recruitment tools exist (Linked Recruiter, Seek Talent Search). A $400k/year cafe owner has nothing between "post on Facebook for free" and "pay $300+ per ad on SEEK."

## Competitive Landscape

| Competitor | Role | Threat | Weakness |
|------------|------|--------|----------|
| **SEEK** | Dominant job board | High — brand, traffic, employer base | Expensive per ad ($300+); form-heavy; bad for casual/shift roles |
| **Indeed** | Aggregator | Medium — free to post, huge reach | Pushes sponsored ads; no swipe UX; same form-heavy apply flow |
| **Jora** | Free job board | Low — used mostly for low-skill roles | Limited employer tools; no mobile-native UX |
| **LinkedIn** | Professional network | Low — wrong audience for casual work | Professional focus; not for barista/hospitality roles |
| **Airtasker** | Gig marketplace | Low-adjacent — task-based, not work-based | One-off tasks vs ongoing shifts; different user psychology |
| **Facebook Groups** | Organic community | Medium — active but messy | No matching, no tracking, spam, hard to scale |
| **Agency staffing** | Traditional | Low-adjacent — expensive, slow | 15-25% cut; slow to fill; don't serve micro-employers |
| **Sidekicker** | Hospitality staffing | Direct — event/hospitality shift matching | Events-focused; agency-style model; expensive for employers |

## Competitive Thesis

Don't compete as "another job board." Compete as:

**"The fastest way to find and fill casual shifts in Melbourne."**

Winning wedge: **speed + simplicity + local focus** beats breadth + features + national coverage.

SEEK and Indeed win on breadth but lose on UX, speed, and relevance for casual work. SwipeJobs wins by being faster, simpler, and Melbourne-focused.

## Beachhead

### First market: Melbourne hospitality + retail casual work

**Why this segment:**
- High volume of casual/shift-based roles
- Employers have recurring, fast-turnaround hiring needs
- Candidates are mobile-native, young, looking for flexible work
- SEEK/Indeed are overkill for a 4-hour bar shift
- This segment has the strongest "need speed" signal

### Secondary (after validation):
- Warehouse/logistics casual roles
- Events/cleaning
- Broader Melbourne casual work

### Avoid in MVP:
- Full-time professional roles
- Remote/regional jobs
- Sydney/beyond

## Differentiation

| Dimension | SEEK/Indeed | SwipeJobs Melbourne |
|-----------|-------------|---------------------|
| Apply flow | Form + resume + cover letter | Swipe interested → employer sees profile |
| Job browsing | List + search + filters | Swipe deck + filters |
| Primary device | Desktop-first | Mobile-first |
| Focus | All jobs everywhere | Casual/shift-based, Melbourne |
| Job cost | $300+/ad | Free / low-cost |
| Time to apply | 10-15 minutes | 10 seconds |
| Matching | Keyword search | Location + availability + category |

## Monetisation Hypothesis

- **Candidates:** Free (always)
- **Employers:** Free to post / Freemium for basic listings. Paid for:
  - Featured/priority job posts
  - Access to interested-candidate contact details
  - Subscription for regular hirers (monthly pass)
  - Agency-style add-ons (optional)

MVP should be free on both sides to validate demand. Monetisation is a Phase 2 concern.

## Why Melbourne

- Single city focus = better matching density
- Local = easier employer outreach (walkable, networkable)
- Strong casual workforce (student population, hospitality industry, events)
- Avoids national marketplace cold start
- Brandable as a local Melbourne product

## Key Questions to Validate

1. Do Melbourne casual workers hate the current apply process enough to try something new?
2. Will small Melbourne employers post jobs on an unproven platform?
3. Do candidates prefer swiping to scrolling?
4. Can we source enough real jobs without scraping?
5. What's the real willingness to pay on the employer side?
