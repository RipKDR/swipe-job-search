# Vision Manifesto — Hi-Hired

> *"The algorithm is you."*

## The Thesis

SEEK was built for one customer: **corporate HR departments.** It optimizes for their needs — keyword-filtered applications, ATS integration, compliance-ready processes — and treats the actual human being looking for work as a data point to be screened, scored, and discarded.

It's a great recruitment tool for enterprise HR.

It's **garbage** if you're a job seeker who just wants to find honest work without playing keyword games. Or a small business owner who needs to hire a barista without paying $300 per ad and wading through 200 irrelevant applications.

## The Problem

The user's experience on SEEK is a gauntlet of friction designed to serve everyone except the two people who actually matter:

| What Job Seekers Actually Want | What SEEK Makes Them Do |
|---|---|
| See the pay upfront | "Competitive salary" — you find out at offer stage, if ever |
| Apply with one tap | Write a profile, upload a resume, write a cover letter, answer screening questions, fill in your education history again |
| Know if the employer is interested | Apply into the void. Maybe hear back. Maybe not. |
| Chat with the employer directly | Go through a formal process, ATS workflow, phone screen |
| Say "no thanks" without drama | Ghost or send a formal withdrawal |

| What Employers Actually Want | What SEEK Makes Them Do |
|---|---|
| Post a job in 2 minutes | Fill in 47 fields, select the right taxonomy, write SEO-optimized copy |
| See who's actually keen | Pay $300+ per ad, get 200 unqualified applicants from keyword-stuffed resumes |
| Talk to candidates directly | Filter through an ATS, use screening questions, wait for HR to process |
| Fill the role fast | Wait for the application period, then screen, then interview, then offer |

## The Insight

**The dating app model already fixed this.**

Tinder, Hinge, Bumble retrained a generation:
- See someone's profile. Swipe right if you like it. Swipe left if you don't.
- Match only if both parties opt in — no unwanted contact.
- Chat when you're both ready — no formalities, no templates.
- Unmatch if it's not right — zero guilt, zero explanation needed.

This same psychology applies perfectly to job seeking. It's not dating — but the *mechanics* of low-friction evaluation, bilateral consent, and direct communication are identical. Swiping on a job card is faster, more honest, and more human than filling out an application for a role you're not even sure you want.

## What Hi-Hired Stands For

### ✅ YOU are the algorithm
No keyword matching. No AI resume screening. No "recommended for you" black box. Job seekers see jobs. They swipe. They decide. The platform gets out of the way.

### ✅ Full transparency, always
Pay rate. Hours. Location. Job type (casual/perm/part-time). All on the card. No "competitive." No "negotiable depending on experience." No "we'll discuss at interview." If it's not on the card, it doesn't get posted.

### ✅ Hi or Hired — nothing in between
Two states. You're either saying **hi** (you swiped, you matched, you chatted — zero commitment) or you're **hired** (offer made, accepted, done). No six-stage interview process for a casual retail role.

### ✅ Bilateral consent
You don't get messaged by employers you didn't opt into. You don't get applications from people you don't want to hear from. Both parties say yes before communication opens.

### ✅ Anti-corporate by design
No cover letters. No screening questions. No "what's your salary expectation" forms. No ATS. No HR workflow. Just people connecting with people about work.

## What We're Against

- ❌ Keyword-driven search that hides jobs unless you guess the right terms
- ❌ Hidden pay — "competitive salary" is a red flag, not a feature
- ❌ Application black holes — submit and never hear back
- ❌ One-sided communication — employers can message you but you can't message them
- ❌ Cover letters. In any form. For any role. Ever.
- ❌ AI resume screening that discards good candidates for not using the right keywords
- ❌ Form-heavy, desktop-first UX on a mobile world
- ❌ $300+ per ad pricing that excludes small businesses

## The North Star

A world where:
- A cafe owner posts their Saturday barista shift — $32/hr, 8am-2pm, Brunswick
- 30 people in the area see it immediately on their phone
- 8 swipe right because the pay and location work for them
- The owner sees 8 faces, reads their quick profiles, picks 2 to chat with
- One match clicks. Both tap "Hired." Done.
- Total time from post to hire: under 30 minutes.
- No ads. No applications. No cover letters. No bullshit.

**Hi-Hired: the job finder that doesn't suck.**
