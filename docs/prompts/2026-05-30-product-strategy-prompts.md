## Core App Vision & Philosophy

This prompt establishes the foundational vision for a Tinder-like job search application targeting the Australian market, with emphasis on transparency, speed, and candidate-first design.

Design a mobile-first job search platform for the Australian market (initially Melbourne-focused) that operates on a Tinder-like swipe interface. The core value propositions are: (1) Transparency — businesses must clearly state if a role is temporary, contract, or permanent so candidates are never misled about job security; (2) Speed — users can get hired quickly or meet their Centrelink/Workforce Australia mutual obligation requirements with minimal friction; (3) Simplicity — avoid feature bloat; focus on what job seekers actually need. The target users are young Australians (ages 18-35) struggling with competitive job markets, staffing mills that cycle through cheap temp labor, and opaque hiring processes. Think about what would make this app indispensable to every job seeker in Australia. Consider the pain points: being ghosted after applications, applying to fake jobs, AI-written resumes masking incompetence, and the administrative burden of proving job search activity to government agencies. Design the MVP feature set around these problems. Do not include features for features' sake. Every element must solve a real problem.


## Anti-Ghosting & Transparency Score

This prompt creates a company/recruiter rating system that discourages ghosting and temp-role churn while empowering job seekers with community-driven insights.

Design an 'Anti-Ghosting & Transparency Score' system for a job search app. After a user completes an application process (whether they get the job or not), prompt them to rate their experience with the recruiter or company across dimensions like: communication quality, timeliness of feedback, whether the job description matched reality, and whether the role was truly available or a 'ghost' listing. Aggregate these ratings into a public-facing Transparency Score for each employer. Companies with high scores attract better talent; companies that churn through temps, post fake jobs, or ghost candidates receive visible penalties. Include a 'Burned' flag — if enough users mark a company as unresponsive, it triggers an automated warning for all other users viewing that company's listings. Think about how to prevent gaming the system (e.g., employers creating fake positive reviews, competitors sabotaging scores). Consider also how this score becomes a B2B selling point: employers with good scores will want to advertise this distinction. The mechanic should feel fair, informative, and genuinely protective of job seekers.


## Vetted Talent Verification

This prompt designs a skill verification badge system that replaces resume fluff with demonstrated competency, creating premium talent tiers advertisers will pay to access.

Design a 'Vetted Talent' verification badge system for a job platform. Instead of relying solely on resume claims, create multi-tier verification levels: (1) Identity Verification — government ID check to confirm the person is real; (2) Skill Verification — role-specific competency tests (e.g., diagnostic scenarios for automotive technicians, coding challenges for developers, situational judgment tests for customer service roles); (3) Reference Verification — automated reference checks that validate past employment claims; (4) Portfolio Review — for creative roles, peer or expert review of work samples. Users who complete verifications receive visible 'Golden Profile' badges. Advertisers (employers) pay a premium to access pre-vetted talent pools because it saves them hours of manual screening and filters out low-quality applications. Think about how to make verification accessible (not expensive or time-consuming) while maintaining rigor. Consider integrations with existing platforms like HackerRank, LeetCode, or GitHub for tech roles. The goal is to create a trusted signal that separates genuine candidates from resume-padders.


## Proof-of-Competency Anti-Bullshitter System

This prompt builds an AI-resistant practical assessment layer that filters candidates who can talk but cannot perform.

Design a 'Proof-of-Competency' layer that filters out candidates who use AI-polished resumes to mask real-world incompetence. The mechanic: when a user applies for a technical role, they must complete a 5-minute interactive 'Work-Sample Simulation' specific to that role. Examples: for a service technician — 'A diagnostic test shows error code P0171 on a vehicle. What is your step-by-step procedure to isolate the faulty sensor?' For a barista — 'A customer orders a triple-shot oat milk latte extra hot, but the oat milk is splitting. What do you do?' Use an LLM to evaluate the response logic against industry-standard troubleshooting guides. If the candidate suggests dangerous, nonsensical, or clearly AI-generated steps, flag their 'Technical Proficiency' score. The system should detect responses that sound professional but lack practical specificity — the hallmark of AI-generated fluff. Consider how to balance rigor with respect: candidates should see this as proof of their skill, not a barrier. Include a practice mode where users can attempt simulations before applying. Think about employer-configurable scenarios that let businesses set their own competency thresholds.


## Centrelink / Workforce Australia Compliance Suite

This prompt automates mutual obligation tracking, turning a legal burden into a user-retention feature.

Design an automated 'Compliance Suite' for Australian job seekers who must meet mutual obligation requirements for Centrelink, Workforce Australia, or employment service providers like Asuria. Every job application submitted through the platform is automatically logged in a timestamped, tamper-proof format. Build a 'Compliance Dashboard' that shows: how many applications the user has submitted this reporting period, which employers were contacted, dates and times of each application, and outcomes where available. Generate downloadable PDF reports that match the exact format required by employment service providers — so users can simply download and submit at their appointments. Include deadline reminders that notify users when their reporting date is approaching and flag if they are below their required application count. Think about optional appointment tracking — users can input upcoming meetings with their job provider and the app will ensure they have enough activity logged beforehand. The goal is to transform compliance from a stressful administrative burden into a seamless, automated process that makes your app a must-use tool for every job seeker in the Australian welfare-to-work system.


## AI Resume Substance Detection

This prompt creates a system that audits AI-generated resumes for vague claims and forces users toward specificity.

Design an AI 'Substance Scanner' that audits uploaded resumes and detects AI-generated fluff. When a user uploads or pastes their resume, the system parses it for vague, generic claims such as 'Improved team efficiency,' 'Excellent communication skills,' or 'Results-driven professional.' For each vague claim detected, the system prompts the user with specific questions to extract concrete evidence: instead of 'Improved team efficiency' — 'Can you quantify this? What was the team output before and after your intervention? By what percentage did productivity change?' Instead of 'Excellent communication skills' — 'Describe a time you had to explain a complex technical issue to a non-technical stakeholder. What was the outcome?' The scanner should differentiate between legitimate, specific achievements and AI-generated filler. Users with substantiated, specific resumes receive a 'Verified Profile' badge that signals quality to employers. Think about tone — the system should feel like a helpful career coach, not a gatekeeper. Include an 'AI Rewrite Assist' that helps users transform vague claims into compelling, specific statements with factual backing. The goal is to raise the quality of every resume on the platform, benefiting both candidates and employers.


## Hyper-Local Job Discovery & Community

This prompt builds location-aware job discovery with community features that reduce the isolation of job hunting.

Design a 'Hyper-Local Job Discovery' system for a Melbourne-focused job platform. Instead of just listing jobs by suburb, map opportunities to real-world 'job hubs' — distinct zones like Melbourne CBD, Cremorne (tech hub), South Melbourne, Docklands, etc. Use GPS-enabled proximity filters so users can set their maximum acceptable commute distance and only see jobs within that radius. Include community features: (1) 'Application Heatmap' — anonymized data showing where other users are applying, helping job seekers understand competition levels in different areas; (2) 'Local Hub Insights' — community-sourced information about specific areas (e.g., 'Cremorne has lots of tech startups but parking is expensive,' 'South Melbourne warehouses often hire same-day for temp work'); (3) 'Commute Calculator' — integrates with public transport APIs to show realistic door-to-door commute times, not just straight-line distance. Think about how to surface 'hidden gem' employers — small businesses that hire frequently but don't advertise on major job boards. Consider hyper-local push notifications: 'You are near the Richmond retail district — 3 new hospitality roles posted in the last hour within a 10-minute walk.' The goal is to make job discovery feel local, immediate, and community-powered rather than cold and algorithmic.


## Inferred Expertise Engine (Passive Profiler)

This prompt designs an automated profile-building system that reduces friction while improving skill representation accuracy.

Design an 'Inferred Expertise Engine' that automatically builds a user's skill profile without requiring manual resume updates. Use OAuth2 integrations with platforms like GitHub (for developers — analyze repositories, languages used, commit frequency, project complexity), StackOverflow (for technical roles — reputation score, tags answered, accepted answers), and LinkedIn (for professional history — endorsements, recommendations, role progression). Use an LLM agent to analyze this data and generate a 'Hard Skill Graph' — a structured, validated representation of the user's actual capabilities. For example, a GitHub scan might reveal: 'Proficient in Python (3 years, 12 repos), Familiar with React (2 years, 4 repos), Learning Rust (1 repo, recent activity).' This solves the 'cold start' problem — new users have a populated profile from day one. It also solves the 'outdated resume' problem — profiles stay current automatically. Think about privacy controls: users must explicitly authorize each integration and can exclude specific repositories or data points. Consider how this becomes a competitive moat: the more integrations a user connects, the richer their profile becomes, and the better the job matching. The goal is to eliminate the friction of manual profile maintenance while dramatically improving the accuracy of skill representation.


## Offer-Potential Forecasting Model

This prompt creates a personalized probability-of-success score that transforms the app from a discovery tool into a strategic career planner.

Design an 'Offer-Potential' forecasting model that shows users the probability of receiving an interview or offer for each job they view. The model uses multiple data signals: (1) the user's skill profile vs. job requirements (skill match percentage); (2) historical data from similar candidates (e.g., 'Candidates with your profile had a 23% interview rate for Senior Backend roles at this company'); (3) market conditions (how long the role has been open, application-to-hire ratios for similar positions); (4) company-specific patterns (some companies interview broadly, others are highly selective). Present the result as a 'Probability of Success' score (e.g., 'High Match — 78% of candidates with your profile received interviews for similar roles'). Include 'Gap Analysis' — if the score is low, explain specifically why: 'Missing: 2 years Kubernetes experience. Low-match factor: Company X typically hires candidates with AWS certifications.' Think about how to present this without discouraging users — the goal is strategic guidance, not gatekeeping. Consider a 'Bridge Path' feature that shows exactly what skills or experiences would move a 'Low Match' to a 'Medium Match.' The goal is to turn the app from a passive job listing viewer into an active career strategy tool.


## Company-Sentiment & Culture Scoring

This prompt builds a pipeline that surfaces real workplace culture information companies prefer to hide, creating deep user trust.

Design a 'Company Culture Confidence Score' that aggregates real employee sentiment from multiple sources. Integrate a sentiment analysis pipeline that scrapes and analyzes discussions from Glassdoor, Blind, Reddit (r/cscareerquestions, r/melbourne, industry-specific subreddits), and Twitter/X for company-specific discourse. Aggregate findings into a structured score across dimensions: work-life balance, management quality, career growth, compensation fairness, and turnover risk. If a user swipes on a job at a company with concerning patterns, display a contextual note: 'Note: 67% of recent employee reviews mention high turnover in this department' or 'Alert: Glassdoor reviews indicate unpaid overtime is common.' Conversely, highlight positive signals: 'Top-rated: 89% of employees recommend this workplace.' Think carefully about fairness: use only recent reviews (last 12 months), weight verified employee reviews higher than anonymous ones, and flag potential review bombing (sudden influx of extreme ratings). Include a 'Trending' indicator showing whether sentiment is improving or declining. The goal is to provide the information companies don't want candidates to know — creating immense user trust and helping job seekers make informed decisions about where they invest their time and energy.


## Career-Pathing Analytics

This prompt creates a long-term career coaching feature that visualizes trajectory and identifies skill gaps.

Design a 'Career-Pathing Analytics' dashboard that visualizes a user's career trajectory and provides actionable guidance. Based on their swipe patterns, saved jobs, and application history, the system infers their career direction: 'Your activity suggests you are pivoting from Front-End Development toward DevOps/Platform Engineering.' Generate a personalized 'Career Map' showing: (1) Current Position — their existing skills and experience level; (2) Target Role — the role they are most frequently engaging with; (3) Skill Gap Analysis — specific skills or certifications needed to make the transition, with estimated time to acquire each; (4) Market Demand — how many relevant roles are posted monthly in their target area; (5) Salary Trajectory — estimated compensation at each step of the path. Include milestone tracking: 'Complete AWS certification (4-6 weeks) to unlock 340% more relevant roles.' Think about how to present this visually — a timeline or roadmap format that feels aspirational, not overwhelming. Consider integration with online learning platforms (Coursera, Udemy, freeCodeCamp) to suggest specific courses that close identified skill gaps. The goal is to transform the app from a short-term job finder into a long-term career development platform that users rely on for years.


## Tailored Apply & One-Swipe Application

This prompt designs the friction-killing application mechanic that removes the pain of selection criteria and cover letters.

Design a 'Tailored Apply' system that eliminates the friction of traditional job applications. When a user swipes right on a job, they have two options: (1) 'Quick Apply' — submits their profile, verified skills, and a concise AI-generated cover letter tailored to that specific job description; (2) 'Custom Apply' — opens a pre-filled application where the AI has already mapped their relevant experience to the job's selection criteria, requiring only review and minor edits. The AI tailoring engine should: extract key requirements from the job description, match them against the user's verified skills and experience, generate specific responses to selection criteria using concrete examples from the user's profile, and maintain the user's authentic voice (not generic AI-speak). For employers, include a 'Application Quality Score' so they can see at a glance whether a candidate has relevant, verified skills. Think about speed — the entire Quick Apply process should take under 10 seconds. Consider also a 'Draft Applications' folder where users can save applications to review later, with automatic reminders before application deadlines. The goal is to make applying to jobs as effortless as swiping, while maintaining application quality that impresses employers.


## Feedback Loops & Rejection Analytics

This prompt turns rejections into learning opportunities by providing specific, actionable feedback.

Design a 'Feedback Loop' system that transforms rejections into learning opportunities. When an employer declines a candidate, require (or strongly encourage) them to select from structured rejection reasons: missing specific certification, insufficient years of experience, skill mismatch in specific area, role already filled, stronger candidate selected, or other (with brief explanation). Present this feedback to the candidate constructively: 'You were not selected because: Missing required certification: AWS Solutions Architect. Recommended action: This certification typically takes 3-4 weeks to complete and would make you eligible for 45% more similar roles.' Aggregate rejection patterns over time to identify systemic gaps: 'Based on your last 10 applications, the most common reason for non-selection is missing project management experience. Consider adding a PMP certification or seeking PM-adjacent roles to bridge this gap.' For employers who provide detailed feedback, offer incentives like reduced advertising fees or 'Top Employer' badges. Think about tone — feedback must be honest but encouraging, never demoralizing. Include a 'Growth Tracker' that shows the user how many of their past feedback items they have since addressed. The goal is to turn every rejection into a concrete step toward the next acceptance.


## Salary Transparency & Collective Leverage

This prompt builds an anonymous salary reporting feature that creates market fairness and positions the app as the source of truth for compensation data.

Design a 'Salary Transparency' feature that empowers job seekers with real market data. After a user accepts a job offer (confirmed through the platform), prompt them to anonymously report: base salary, any bonuses or commissions, benefits (superannuation contribution, flexible work, professional development budget), and role level/title. Aggregate this data to show: (1) 'Market Rate' ranges for each role type in each location (e.g., 'Junior Developer in Melbourne CBD: $65K-$78K base'); (2) 'Your Position' — where the user's offer sits relative to the market (below average, average, above average); (3) 'Negotiation Support' — if a user's offer is below market, suggest negotiation talking points based on verified market data from the platform. Protect anonymity: never show individual submissions, only aggregated data with minimum sample sizes (e.g., data only shown when 5+ submissions exist for a role type). Think about legal compliance with Australian privacy laws. Consider employer-facing features: anonymized salary benchmarking reports they can purchase to ensure their offers are competitive. The goal is to make your app the 'source of truth' for salary data in the Australian job market — creating a compelling reason for every job seeker to use the platform before accepting any offer.


## Disrupting the Staffing Mill Economy

This prompt creates the overarching market positioning that frames the app as a market correction tool, not just another job board.

Design a comprehensive 'Anti-Staffing Mill' strategy for a job platform targeting the Melbourne market. The core problem: businesses exploit young job seekers through a cycle of 'rinse and repeat' temp hiring — they advertise permanent positions, hire temps with no intention of conversion, extract cheap labor for 3-6 months, then replace them. Your platform must systematically dismantle this model. Design the following mechanics: (1) 'Role Truth Verification' — employers must specify contract type at posting; the system monitors whether perm roles actually convert to perm employment, flagging discrepancies. (2) 'Churn Alerts' — if a company has hired and not retained 3+ temps for the same role in 6 months, display a warning: 'High turnover detected: This company has cycled 4 temps through this position in 5 months.' (3) 'Conversion Tracking' — for temp-to-perm roles, track actual conversion rates by company and surface this data to candidates. (4) 'Worker Protection' — partner with relevant unions or worker advocacy groups to provide resources for exploited temps. (5) 'Ethical Employer Certification' — companies that demonstrate fair hiring practices earn a trusted badge. Think about the business model: how do you attract ethical employers while filtering out staffing mills? Consider premium pricing for high-churn employers to disincentivize bad behavior. The platform's brand positioning should be clear: 'We are not a job board. We are a market correction tool that puts candidates first.'
